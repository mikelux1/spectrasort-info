The gap the desktop tools leave

Serious culling software lives on the desktop, and much of it is good — genre-trained AI, tethered workflows, XMP handoff to Lightroom. It also assumes the desk: a laptop in the loop, an import step before you can even look, and in many cases a subscription running whether you shot this month or not.

Meanwhile the actual photos are often already on your phone — AirDropped from the camera, pulled through an SD-card reader, or synced by the camera's own app. iPhone is where you look at them first. SpectraSort's argument is simple: the first cull should happen right there, and it shouldn't cost a monthly fee.

The working surface

A contact sheet that arrives already ranked.

Point a sort at your import album and every frame comes back scored against your criteria, strongest first — a contact sheet where the top row is the shortlist.

The capture here is a real pass over a 650-frame architecture shoot. It came back in a moment: sorts run on an on-device index that's built once and kept current as new photos appear.

From there it's a photographer's review: swipe the selects in, roll through near-identical takes on one wheel, or let Autopick file the whole set by score and skim the result.

Why Studio is the photographer's half of the app

One-tap scoring is built for the average camera roll. A shoot isn't average — you know the subject, you know the look, and "keeper" has a definition. Studio lets you write that definition down:

  • Seven quality axes, hand-tuned on a radar — or slid together from Technical to Artistic. Your noise tolerance, your composition bar.
  • Up to three subjects per sort, so a street shoot can weigh Portrait + Street + Night at once.
  • The balance wheel, dialing how much subject match counts against raw execution.
  • Saved profiles — the part that makes it a system. Tune once per shoot type; every future shoot gets identical standards.
  • An album filter, so the sort judges only your imports, not the whole roll.

In the developer's own camera workflow, this combination — tuned radar, named subjects, balance set, saved as a profile — is what turns good-enough picks into reliable ones.

The workflow, end to end

  1. Import as you already do. AirDrop, SD-card reader, or your camera maker's app — RAW or JPG+RAW both come in through iOS's own import, and a pair shows up as one photo. Once the frames are in the photo library, they're indexed automatically.
  2. Filter to the import album so the sort sees only the shoot.
  3. Load your profile for that shoot type — or tune one and save it, once.
  4. Sort. The ranked review comes back in a moment, strongest frames first, near-identicals stacked.
  5. Review like a cull: right to pick, down to keep, left to let go. Stacks settle on one wheel; Autopick files everything by score if you'd rather skim.
  6. Batch Process: selects to an album (visible in Apple Photos and anything that syncs with it), favorites marked, rejects cleared through the system dialog.
What this is not — so you're not surprised
  • It culls your photo library, not memory cards — import first, cull second. iOS's own import handles the files fine, RAW included; the cull just happens after they're in the library, not off the card.
  • Selections come out as albums, favorites and deletions in your library — not XMP sidecars. Albums and favorites do carry through iCloud into anything that reads your library.
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later.

FAQ

Photographer questions.

Does it work with photos from my mirrorless or DSLR?
It works with whatever is in your iPhone photo library. If your camera's frames land there — AirDrop, an SD-card reader, or the camera maker's transfer app — SpectraSort indexes and ranks them like any other photo, and the album filter can restrict a sort to just those imports.
Does it work with RAW files?
Yes, once they're in your library. iOS's own import brings in RAW or JPG+RAW — a pair is treated as a single photo — and SpectraSort indexes and ranks those like any other frame. What it doesn't do is read cards directly: the workflow is import first, cull second.
How do my selects get to Lightroom or my desktop?
As library structure, not sidecar files: Batch Process puts picks in an album and can mark favorites, and those carry through iCloud Photos into Apple Photos everywhere and any tool that reads your library. In Lightroom on iPhone, a favorited photo comes through with its select flag already on. There's no XMP export.
Is it fast enough for a 2,000-frame shoot?
Sorting is near-instant once frames are indexed, and indexing is automatic — up to 100 photos a second on recent iPhones, picked up as imports appear. The review is the real work, and ranking plus stacked near-identicals is what keeps it short.
Is the free tier enough for shoot work?
Five full sorts a week, free forever — fine for the occasional shoot. If you're culling regularly, Premium is $19.99 once for unlimited sorts plus Studio's full controls, which is the half built for camera work. No subscription.

The questions to ask any AI photo app

  • Where does the AI run? “In the cloud” means your photos travel. “On-device” means they don't.
  • Is there an account? An account is a database row with your name on it. No account, nothing to breach.
  • Are your photos used for training? If they're uploaded, read the terms twice.
  • Does the privacy claim match the App Privacy label? The label is audited by listing; marketing pages aren't.

SpectraSort's answers: on-device, no account, never trained on, and the label agrees.

Architecture, not policy

Private because it can't be otherwise.

A privacy policy is a promise; an architecture is a fact. SpectraSort indexes, searches and scores on the iPhone's Neural Engine, and your taste profile is built and stored on the device. There is no SpectraSort server in the path at all.

The models ship pre-trained and do not learn from users' photos — so the developer has no way to access your photo content, promise or no promise. Apple's App Privacy label on the listing corroborates this.

It also means sorting doesn't need a connection: the pipeline is local.

The one network thing, disclosed

Full transparency: the app offers anonymous usage analytics (Firebase Analytics + Crashlytics) — events like “sort started” plus crash diagnostics, never photo content and never locations. It's opt-in, the toggle defaults to off in the EU, UK and EEA, and it can be turned off anywhere in Settings → Privacy.

That's the entire network footprint. There's nothing else to disclose, which is rather the point.

What on-device buys you beyond privacy

  • No account — download and sort. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to remember.
  • No connection needed — the sorting pipeline runs locally, wherever you are.
  • No subscription — no server costs to pass on monthly. Five sorts a week free forever; $19.99 once for unlimited.
  • Speed — the index is built once, at up to 100 photos a second on recent iPhones; every sort after is near-instant.
Where it doesn't help — honestly
  • On-device also means the work happens on your chip: the first index of a very large library takes real time, and newer iPhones index faster than older ones.
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No iPad, Android or desktop version.
  • The first index of a very large library takes a while — it's a one-time cost.
  • Deleted photos sit in iOS's Recently Deleted for 30 days, so storage comes back later — SpectraSort won't show a fake “GB freed” number.

FAQ

Privacy questions.

Do my photos ever leave my phone?
No. The search, the scoring and the taste profile all run on your iPhone's Neural Engine. There are no uploads, no account, and no SpectraSort server in the path.
Does SpectraSort use my photos to train AI?
No. Nothing is uploaded, so there is nothing to train on. The models ship pre-trained, and the maker never sees your photos.
Can the developer see my photos?
No — there's no technical path for it. Processing is local, there's no account, and Apple's App Privacy label on the listing corroborates the claim.
Does it work without an internet connection?
Sorting does — the pipeline is entirely local. You'll need a connection to download the app and for optional things like the App Store purchase.
Is there really no subscription?
Really. Five sorts a week are free forever; Premium is $19.99 exactly once, tied to your Apple account so it restores on a new phone.

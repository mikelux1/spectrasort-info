Three kinds of "duplicate"

  • Exact copies — same file saved twice. Apple Photos' built-in Duplicates album detects and merges these (and near-exact variants) already.
  • Bursts and retakes: many frames of the same moment, seconds apart, none of them byte-identical. Built-in tools leave these alone, and they make up most of the clutter.
  • Look-alikes: the same shot of the same skyline, taken months apart. No exact-match scan will ever connect them.

SpectraSort's Dedupe tool targets all three, because it compares what's in the photos, not the files.

Stacked, scored, settled

Each repeated moment costs one decision.

Dedupe hands you stacks, not a list. Each group of repeats comes with the sharpest frame flagged; you accept the flag or pick a different keeper, and the stack is settled.

The screenshot is from a real pass: 315 photos pulled into stacks, most of them pairs, a few a dozen deep, and one rose photographed 19 times. Each stack shows its count and its top score.

Inside a stack, the takes sit on a wheel, so you can roll through them without leaving the photo. When you're done, one batch clears the rejects through the system confirmation dialog. Nothing is deleted without your tap.

Straight answers about storage

Cleaning apps love a big “you freed 4.2 GB!” number. On iOS that number isn't true at the moment of deletion: photos go to the system's Recently Deleted album first. Empty it there and the space comes back immediately; leave it and iOS clears it after 30 days. SpectraSort tells you that instead of showing a figure it can't stand behind.

It also touches only your photo library, never the rest of your phone. The comparison itself runs on the iPhone's Neural Engine, so your photos are never uploaded to be scanned.

Where it won't help
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No iPad, Android or desktop version.
  • The very first index of a big library takes some time. After that it's automatic — new photos are indexed as they appear, so you don't wait again.
  • Deleted photos go through iOS's own trash: they land in Recently Deleted, and the storage comes back the moment you empty that album there — or automatically after 30 days. SpectraSort doesn't show a “GB freed” number because iOS controls that timing.

FAQ

Duplicate questions.

How is this different from Apple's built-in duplicate finder?
Apple Photos' Duplicates album merges exact and near-exact copies of the same shot. SpectraSort also stacks bursts, retakes and look-alikes, which are different files of the same moment, and flags the sharpest in each stack. Those account for most of the clutter.
Can it delete photos without asking?
No. Nothing is applied until you tap, and every deletion goes through the system Photos confirmation dialog into Recently Deleted, where it stays for 30 days.
Does deleting duplicates free up storage right away?
As fast as you choose. Deleted photos land in iOS's Recently Deleted album; empty it and the space comes back immediately, or iOS clears it on its own after 30 days. That's the system's safety net, not a SpectraSort quirk, and the app says so instead of showing an inflated number.
Can it find similar photos, not just duplicates?

Is the duplicate finder free?

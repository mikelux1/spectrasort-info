iOS finds one kind of duplicate. There are three.

Duplicates — exact copies of the same file. Bursts — shots seconds apart, none of them byte-identical. Similar — the same scene, shot again months later.

Apple Photos merges the first kind. The other two are most of the clutter. SpectraSort compares what's in the picture, so all three land in stacks.

One stack, one decision

19 takes → one keeper.

Inside a stack the takes sit on a wheel. Roll through them without leaving the photo, keep the one you want, and the stack is done.

Or let Autopick do it: the copies go to Discard and the best of every stack stays for you to decide. Then one batch clears the rejects — behind the system confirmation dialog, into Recently Deleted.

How it works
Scan
Dedupe reads the on-device index — built once, then kept current as new photos arrive. Up to 100 photos a second on the first pass.
Pick
Sharpest flagged in every stack. Confirm it, or roll the wheel and choose another.
Clear
One batch. Deletions go through the system dialog into Recently Deleted.

Clearing duplicates is where most of a camera roll's storage goes. Deleted photos land in iOS's Recently Deleted album; empty it there and the space comes back at once, or leave it and iOS frees it after 30 days. SpectraSort doesn't show a “GB freed” number, because iOS controls that timing.

Where it won't help
  • It looks at your photo library only — not files, downloads or anything else on the phone.
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No iPad, Android or desktop version.
  • The very first index of a big library takes some time. After that it's automatic — new photos are indexed as they appear, so you don't wait again.
  • Deleted photos go through iOS's own trash: they land in Recently Deleted, and the storage comes back the moment you empty that album there — or automatically after 30 days.

FAQ

Duplicate questions.

How is this different from Apple's built-in duplicate finder?
Apple Photos' Duplicates album merges exact and near-exact copies of the same shot. SpectraSort also stacks bursts, retakes and look-alikes — different files of the same moment — and flags the sharpest in each stack. Those are most of the clutter.
Can it delete photos without asking?
No. Nothing changes until you tap, and every deletion goes through the system Photos confirmation dialog into Recently Deleted, where it stays for 30 days.
Does deleting duplicates free up storage right away?
Deleted photos land in iOS's Recently Deleted album. Empty that album and the space comes back immediately; leave it and iOS clears it after 30 days. iOS controls that timing, which is why SpectraSort doesn't show a “you freed N GB” number.
Can I choose what counts as a duplicate?
Yes. Three stacking switches in Review: Duplicates (exact copies), Bursts (shots seconds apart) and Similar (the same scene). Turn any of them off and those photos show as singles.
Can it find similar photos, not just duplicates?
Same engine. Like This Photo ranks the whole library by similarity to one picture you choose; the Similar switch groups look-alikes into stacks inside any sort. Three ways to find your best photos →
Is the duplicate finder free?
Dedupe is one of the six tools, and five sorts a week are free forever. Premium ($19.99 once, no subscription) removes the weekly cap.

Three kinds of "duplicate" — only one gets found by default

  • Exact copies — same file saved twice. Apple Photos' built-in Duplicates album detects and merges these (and near-exact variants) already.
  • Bursts and retakes — eleven frames of the same moment, seconds apart, none of them byte-identical. Built-in tools leave these alone; they're most of the clutter.
  • Look-alikes — the same shot of the same skyline, taken months apart. Invisible to any exact-match scan.

SpectraSort's Dedupe tool targets all three, because it compares what's in the photos, not the files.

Stacked, scored, settled

Each repeated moment costs one decision.

Duplicates, bursts and look-alikes arrive already grouped into stacks with the sharpest frame flagged. You pick the keeper — or accept the flag — and the stack is settled.

Near-identical frames sit on a single wheel: roll between them without leaving the photo, compare, take the one you want.

Then one batch: keepers stay, the rest cleared through the system confirmation dialog. Nothing is ever deleted without your tap.

Straight answers about storage

Cleaning apps love a big “you freed 4.2 GB!” number. On iOS it isn't true at the moment of deletion: deleted photos move to Recently Deleted and the space comes back when that album empties — after 30 days, or when you empty it yourself. SpectraSort tells you that instead of showing a number it can't stand behind.

It also only ever touches your photo library — never the rest of your phone. And the comparing happens on the iPhone's Neural Engine: your photos are never uploaded to be scanned.

Where it doesn't help — honestly
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No iPad, Android or desktop version.
  • The first index of a very large library takes a while — it's a one-time cost.
  • Deleted photos sit in iOS's Recently Deleted for 30 days, so storage comes back later — SpectraSort won't show a fake “GB freed” number.

FAQ

Duplicate questions.

How is this different from Apple's built-in duplicate finder?
Apple Photos' Duplicates album merges exact and near-exact copies of the same shot. SpectraSort also stacks bursts, retakes and look-alikes — frames that are different files of the same moment — and flags the sharpest in each stack. That's where most of the clutter actually is.
Can it delete photos without asking?
No. Nothing is applied until you tap, and every deletion goes through the system Photos confirmation dialog into Recently Deleted, where it stays for 30 days.
Does deleting duplicates free up storage right away?
Not immediately — that's an iOS behavior, not an app choice. Deleted photos sit in Recently Deleted for up to 30 days before the space is reclaimed, unless you empty that album yourself. SpectraSort is upfront about this instead of showing an inflated “GB freed” figure.
Can it find similar photos, not just duplicates?
Yes — that's the same engine. Point Like This Photo at any picture to rank the whole library by similarity to it. Three ways to find your best photos →
Is the duplicate finder free?
Dedupe is one of the six tools, and five sorts a week are free forever. Premium ($19.99 once, no subscription) removes the weekly cap.

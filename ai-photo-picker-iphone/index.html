What a photo picker is

The term gets used loosely, so it helps to separate three kinds of app:

  • Photo search finds matches. Ask for "beach" and you get all 87 beach photos you've ever taken, in one long grid, in date order.
  • Photo cleaners look for junk: duplicates, screenshots, blurry shots. Useful, but they can only tell you what's bad.
  • A photo picker makes the call. It scores each photo on sharpness, exposure, composition and how well it matches what you asked for, then hands you the set ranked, best first, with a way to act on it.

Most people don't struggle to find their photos. They struggle to choose between them. SpectraSort is built for the choosing.

Ranked, not just matched

Every result comes back best-first.

There are six tools, one tap each: Your Taste, Best Shots, Find a Subject, Like This Photo, Dedupe, and Photos of a location.

Whichever one you use, results come back scored and ordered. The capture here is a real sort: a 650-photo street slice, every frame scored, best first.

The first sort indexes your library, at up to 100 photos a second on recent iPhones. After that, sorts run against the index and come back almost instantly.

The part no other app does

It learns which photos you'd keep.

Most photo AI scores against a universal idea of a good photo: sharp, well lit, centered. Your favorites don't always fit that mold. Maybe you like the backlit one, the dog mid-shake, or a slightly soft frame you'd still print.

So SpectraSort watches what you keep and what you let go during review, and builds a taste profile from it. After enough reviews, sorting by Your Taste starts to look like an album you picked by hand. You can pause the learning, reset it, or keep several profiles, and it all stays on your phone.

Nothing leaves the phone

All of the scoring happens on your iPhone's Neural Engine. Your photos aren't uploaded anywhere, there's no account, and the models ship pre-trained, so nothing about your library ever reaches anyone else. More on how the on-device setup works →

Where it won't help
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No iPad, Android or desktop version.
  • The first index of a big library takes a while. It only happens once.
  • The taste profile is only as good as the reviews you've given it. It starts generic.
  • Deleted photos spend 30 days in iOS's Recently Deleted album, so the storage comes back later, not right away. SpectraSort doesn't show a "GB freed" number because it would be a guess.

FAQ

AI photo picker questions.

What is an AI photo picker?
An app that decides which of your photos are worth keeping, not just which ones match a search. It scores each photo on sharpness, exposure, composition, or resemblance to the photos you usually keep, and returns them ranked best-first, with a workflow to keep, share or clear the rest.
How is that different from photo search?
Search tells you which photos match, so a beach query returns all 87 beach photos. A picker ranks those 87 and puts the best ones first. SpectraSort does both; every search comes back ranked. See picker vs culling for the full breakdown.
Does SpectraSort upload my photos to pick them?
No. The search, the scoring and the taste profile all run on your iPhone's Neural Engine. No uploads, no account, no SpectraSort server in the path.
Will it delete photos on its own?
No. Nothing is applied until you tap, and deletions always go through the system Photos confirmation dialog. Deleted photos sit in Recently Deleted for 30 days before the space is reclaimed.
How much does it cost?
Five sorts a week are free, forever — not a trial. Premium is $19.99 once for unlimited sorts plus Studio's full controls. No subscription.

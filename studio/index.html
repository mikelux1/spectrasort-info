Studio. For when the defaults aren't yours.

Most sorts never need it: pick a tool, tap, review, done. Studio exists for the libraries that get shot in volume — a camera import two thousand frames deep, or a phone that comes home from every trip with three hundred takes and a promise to deal with them later — where you know exactly what a keeper looks like and want the sort to use your definition.

Two kinds of shooting

A casual roll mostly needs deciding at all: pick a tool, review the ranked result, done. If that's your library, honestly, you can stop reading — the one-tap tools will carry you.

Shooting in volume is a different job — and it doesn't matter whether the frames come off a mirrorless two thousand at a time or off your own phone, held down at a concert because it's easier to shoot now and decide later. Either way they arrive in bulk, and "good" means something specific: your subject, your look, your tolerance for noise against your demand for composition. Generic scoring gets you close. It doesn't get you reliable. Studio is where you make the sort yours — and once it's saved as a profile, every batch after gets the same treatment.

The quality signature

Seven axes. Your weighting.

Sharpness, exposure, contrast, noise, composition, colour harmony, visual balance — each one hand-tunable on the radar, or moved together along a single Technical ↔ Artistic slider.

Crisp documentary work wants the technical end. Mood and light want the artistic one. The radar is your definition of a keeper, drawn once.

Point it at everything, or use the album filter to aim at just the album your imports land in.

The balance wheel

Subject against quality, on one dial.

Every sort is a trade between what's in the frame and how well it's shot. The wheel makes the trade explicit: roll toward your subjects and the sort favors the frames that match them; roll toward quality and it favors the best-executed frames, whatever they show.

Name up to three subjects per sort and weight them as a group. A street shoot might run Portrait + Street + Night at 75% subject; a landscape pass might run 80% quality and let the radar decide.

Profiles are the point

Any one sort can be tuned by hand. What makes Studio a workflow is that the tuning keeps: save the radar, the subjects and the balance as a named profile, and every future shoot of that kind gets judged by the same standards. Consistency is what separates a culling system from a lucky pass.

Profiles live alongside Your Taste, the learned profile built from your keeps and rejects. Taste learns what you like; Studio lets you declare it. Serious libraries tend to use both — taste for the everyday roll, a hand-built profile per shoot type.

How this gets used on a real shoot

From the developer's own workflow, culling camera shoots imported to an iPhone: filter to the import album, load the profile for that kind of shoot — radar drawn for it, subjects named, balance set — and sort. The ranked review comes back in a moment, the swipes settle it, and Batch Process files the keepers. The profile is why the results hold up shoot after shoot: the criteria never drift.

Where it won't help
  • Studio rewards knowing what you want — it's more dials than the one-tap tools, on purpose. If the defaults already pick well for you, you may never need it.
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No iPad, Android or desktop version.
  • The very first index of a big library takes some time. After that it's automatic — new photos are indexed as they appear, so you don't wait again.
  • Deleted photos go through iOS's own trash: they land in Recently Deleted, and the storage comes back the moment you empty that album there — or automatically after 30 days. SpectraSort doesn't show a “GB freed” number because iOS controls that timing.

FAQ

Studio questions.

Is Studio included in the free tier?
Studio's full controls — the seven quality axes, up to three subjects, the balance wheel, and saved profiles — are part of Premium: $19.99 once, no subscription. The one-tap tools and five sorts a week stay free forever.
What are the seven quality axes?
Sharpness, exposure, contrast, noise, composition, colour harmony and visual balance. Weight each one individually on the radar, or move them together along a Technical-to-Artistic slider.
How is a Studio profile different from Your Taste?
Your Taste is learned — it takes shape automatically from the photos you keep and reject. A Studio profile is declared — you set the axes, subjects and balance by hand and save the configuration. Taste suits the everyday roll; hand-built profiles suit repeatable shoot types. Both live on your device.
Can I aim Studio at just my camera imports?
Yes. The album filter restricts a sort to a chosen album, so if your imports land in one place, Studio judges only those frames.
Does any of this leave my phone?
No. Studio's scoring runs on the iPhone's Neural Engine like everything else in SpectraSort — no uploads, no account, no server in the path.

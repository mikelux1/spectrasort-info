How to pick the best photos from your camera roll.

A working method for turning thousands of photos into the few hundred you actually care about — first by hand, then the fast way. Both are real methods; one just scales.

The manual method (no app required)

  1. Work in slices, never the whole library. One trip, one month, one event. A finished slice is progress; a half-scrolled library is not.
  2. Two passes. First pass, cut the obvious failures fast — blur, accidental shots, duds. Second pass, choose between the similars that remain.
  3. Settle near-identicals side by side. Open the burst, compare, keep exactly one. Don't defer — deferred stacks are why the library got like this.
  4. Mark keepers as Favorites (or move them to an album) so the decision is recorded somewhere.
  5. Finish the slice — album filled, rejects deleted or ignored — before opening the next one.

This genuinely works. It costs roughly an evening per thousand photos, which is the honest reason most libraries never get done.

Why it breaks at scale

Three compounding problems: every photo costs a full-price decision because nothing is ranked; near-identicals are scattered, so comparisons mean hunting; and decision fatigue sets in long before the slice ends. The method is sound — the economics aren't.

The assisted version

Same method, pre-sorted.

SpectraSort runs the same five steps with the expensive parts done before you look. Pick a slice — a Scene, a time range, or a subject search — and it comes back ranked, best first, with near-identicals already stacked.

Then the review: swipe right to pick, down to keep, left to let go — or let Autopick choose and skim its picks. Stacked similars sit on one wheel, so a burst costs one decision.

Details that keep it safe

A down-swipe simply keeps a photo — declining to pick something never deletes it. Deletions happen only in Batch Process, go through the system confirmation dialog, and land in Recently Deleted for 30 days. And the ranking runs on your iPhone's Neural Engine — nothing is uploaded.

Where it doesn't help — honestly
  • The method still ends with your judgment — SpectraSort shortens the review; it doesn't take the decisions from you.
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No iPad, Android or desktop version.
  • The first index of a very large library takes a while — it's a one-time cost.
  • Deleted photos sit in iOS's Recently Deleted for 30 days, so storage comes back later — SpectraSort won't show a fake “GB freed” number.

FAQ

Method questions.

How often should I go through my camera roll?
Little and often beats heroic purges. A slice a week — last week's photos, or one old trip — keeps the library decided. SpectraSort's free tier (five sorts a week) is sized for exactly this rhythm.
What should I do with the keepers?
Record the decision: an album, Favorites, or share them somewhere. SpectraSort's Batch Process does any of these in one step, and its albums appear in Apple Photos.
How do I avoid deleting something I'll regret?
Three safety nets: declining to pick a photo never deletes it; deletions require the system confirmation dialog; and deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days, recoverable.
Can I do this without any app?
Yes — the manual method at the top of this page needs nothing but Photos and patience. The app version exists because the patience is usually the missing part.

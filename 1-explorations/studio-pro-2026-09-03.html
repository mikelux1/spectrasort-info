Seven axes

Your definition of sharp, exposed and composed.

Every photo is scored on seven axes. In the one-tap tools the weights are fixed. In Studio each one is yours to set — or slide all seven at once from Technical to Artistic.

  • SharpnessTechnicalFocus and fine detail where it matters — eyes, edges, texture.
  • CompositionArtisticWhere the subject sits in the frame and how the frame is filled.
  • ExposureTechnicalHighlights held, shadows open, nothing clipped.
  • ColorArtisticHow the colors in the frame work with each other.
  • ContrastTechnicalSeparation between light and dark, tone by tone.
  • BalanceArtisticThe visual weight of the frame, left to right and top to bottom.
  • NoiseTechnicalGrain and sensor noise, most visible in the shadows.
Subjects and the wheel

Three subjects. One balance.

Put up to three subjects on a sort — a word, a theme, or a photo. Then turn the wheel: at 75% it hunts the subject and forgives the frame; at 25% it wants the best frame and only nods at the subject.

The loop is a real sort: Portrait, Street and Nature over 650 photos, the wheel turned from 75 / 25 to 25 / 75 and back.

Profiles

One setup per kind of shoot.

Save any Studio setup as a named profile. Bookmark the ones you reach for, pick one per sort, and the Your Taste profile the app learned from your own keeps opens here too — nudge its axes like any other.

The capture: a City & Urban profile — Architecture, Street, Night at 82% subject weight — pointed at one album of 1,708 photos.

Where it fits in a working cull

Studio sorts whatever is in the iPhone's Photos library — the phone's own shots, and camera files that have been imported or synced into it. Picks end as an album, as Favorites, or in the share sheet, so they show up wherever that library already does.

Where it won't help
  • iPhone only, iOS 18.5 or later. No Mac, Windows or iPad version, and no tethered capture.
  • No ratings or color labels are written back into a desktop catalog. The hand-off is an album or a Favorites star.
  • Photos have to be in the Photos library first. A card still in the camera is out of reach.
  • The very first index of a big library takes some time. After that it's automatic — new photos are indexed as they appear.

FAQ

Studio questions.

What does the Technical / Artistic slider actually move?
All seven axes at once. Technical pulls weight toward sharpness, exposure, contrast and noise; Artistic pulls it toward composition, color and balance. Any single axis can still be set on its own afterwards.
Can a photo be a subject?
Yes. A subject is a word, a theme, or a photo — up to three per sort. With a photo, the sort ranks by resemblance to it, the same engine as Like This Photo.
Does Studio work on the profile the app learned from me?
Yes. Your Taste opens in Studio like any saved profile, so you can see what it learned and adjust the axes. Changes save as a new profile; the learned one keeps learning.
Is Studio free?
Opening it and setting up a sort is free. Running a Studio sort is Premium — $19.99 once, which also removes the five-sorts-a-week limit. No subscription.
